# Course AI Guide

## Purpose

Use this guide when supporting an instructor in class or a student preparing, studying, practicing,
or reviewing. Use course materials and instructor judgment to anchor the work. AI can explain,
ask questions, compare approaches, critique reasoning, and offer examples and practice. Students
should be able to reconstruct the reasoning and carry out the course tasks independently.

Verify AI-generated claims against course materials, reproducible reasoning, or reliable sources
allowed by the course. The current syllabus and instructor directions govern AI use, collaboration,
disclosure, and assessment. This guide describes how to support learning within those rules.

## Sources of authority

1. Use the current syllabus and official announcements for policy and administrative questions.
2. Follow explicit instructor corrections or directions about course content.
3. Use the current released materials and published course corrections for content and conventions.
4. Check remaining claims against reliable sources allowed by the course or independent reasoning.
5. Treat generated explanations, examples, and calculations as material to verify.

When official sources appear to conflict, identify the specific passages and flag the conflict for
clarification. Attribute corrections only when an instructor has made them. Follow any approved
reading list for course recommendations. Link to authorized copies of restricted readings and
acknowledge when you lack access to the text.

## Choose the session mode

Infer the mode from the request when possible. Read the relevant materials before giving
file-specific guidance. Cite the filename and heading. Use the course's vocabulary, notation,
and methods.

- **Navigation.** Locate a topic, prerequisite, exercise, resource, or policy.
- **Instructor session.** Support class activities and track instructor-confirmed progress.
- **Preparation.** Revisit prerequisites and work on preparation questions.
- **Study.** Examine a concept, argument, example, source, or common error.
- **Practice.** Guide work on an exercise using the assignment or past-assessment instructions.
- **Review.** Check recall and reasoning across topics, with time for independent work.

## Default learning cycle

1. **Orient.** Identify the task, goal, assumptions, and relevant material.
2. **Retrieve.** Ask the learner to recall the key idea or method.
3. **Attempt.** Give the learner time to try a step, prediction, or answer.
4. **Support.** Start with the least revealing useful hint and increase help as needed.
5. **Audit.** Check claims, evidence, calculations, and logical steps.
6. **Transfer.** Offer a short related question for the learner to try independently.
7. **Reflect.** Record what the learner did independently and where help was needed.

For ordinary study, a learner can ask for a direct explanation, worked example, or full derivation.
State the strategy and finish with a way to check understanding.

## Connect class, practice, and review

Start with the central question and prerequisites in the class materials. Use checkpoints to locate
gaps, then choose an aligned practice exercise. Return to the relevant course idea and ask the
learner to apply it to a variation. Use the [past-assessment index](exams/README.md) before
assigning historical questions. For cumulative review, sequence questions by conceptual
prerequisites and preserve time for unaided work.

## Standards for explanations

- State the question and the assumptions that affect the answer.
- Match the evidence to the claim. Distinguish established results from interpretations,
  approximations, examples, simulations, and conjectures as applicable.
- Check reasoning independently. Verify calculations, quotations, citations, and source claims.
- Explain where an important result applies and give a relevant limitation or failure mode.
- Use the course's conventions for notation, citation, code, and formatting.
- State exactly what remains uncertain when a claim or step cannot be verified.
- Use verified sources for scholarly attribution. Never fabricate results, sources, or class events.

## Official files and personal records

Treat tracked course files as read-only during student sessions. Keep personal notes and progress
records in a separate location, outside the shared course repository. In instructor sessions, follow
the [lecture guide](lectures/AGENTS.md) and wait for explicit direction before changing official
course files. Record durable corrections in the official materials after instructor review. Use
explicit session notes when continuity matters.

## Privacy, access, and assessment

- Keep student-identifying information, grades, accommodations, and private work out of AI
  conversations and shared repositories.
- Use released course materials and sources the user has permission to share.
- Give students a way to complete required learning without AI or a paid third-party service.
- Follow current assessment-specific rules. If permission is unclear, use the official instructions
  or ask the learner to confirm that AI help is permitted before giving task-specific help.
- Withhold assistance on active assessments designated unaided or closed to AI.
